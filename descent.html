07 · The idea
0 × 0

A website that feels like going under.

The Descent is a concept site for an aquarium of the deep. I wanted the first few seconds to feel like the moment you lean over the edge of a boat and look down.

So the whole site is one long dive. You start on calm white. Scroll, and an octopus swims up to meet you. The name settles right on the water line, half above and half below, while a little ship drifts across the top. Keep going and it gets darker, deeper and stranger, all the way down to an anglerfish with its light on.

09 · Art direction
0 × 0

An aquarium for grown-ups, too.

Most aquarium websites are built for kids. Bright primary colors, cartoon fish, bubbles everywhere, every section shouting for attention. They’re fun, but they all look the same, and none of them feel like the hushed, blue wonder of actually standing in front of a tank.

I wanted The Descent to feel like that moment instead. Engaging and interactive, so it still feels like play, but calm, clean and a little bit expensive. Think gallery, not theme park. Lots of white space, one confident typeface, a palette taken straight from the water, and the animals doing the talking.

Classy, not childish.No cartoons, no rainbow. Black type on white, then the deep blues of the sea.
Interactive, not busy.One thing moves at a time: the octopus, the water, the ship. You set the pace by scrolling.
Real animals as the art.Big, crisp creatures on clean backgrounds, like specimens in a museum.

19 · How I built it
0 × 0

How I built it.

One page, one scroll.The home page is a single story driven by scroll position, so every section can hand off to the next instead of just stacking up.
The octopus is 361 frames.I cut the octopus out of a film, frame by frame, and draw it on a canvas so it follows your scroll without a background.
The water is drawn live.The waves, the ship and the specks in the water are code, so they move on their own and the ship rocks when you poke it.
The creatures and films.I made the exhibit animals and the visit film with AI image and video tools, using real photos as references, then cut each one out by hand.
Every link knows where to land.Nav links glide right to their part of the dive, and the logo takes you back to the name on the water.

07 · The question
0 × 0

Snoot started with the question every dog owner Googles at midnight: is this normal?

Nobody can answer it, because nobody has the baseline. A dog’s records live in a vet portal, an email thread and a paper folder, and the small changes that matter most, eating a little less, sleeping a little more, never get written down anywhere. So owners either panic or wait.

Snoot is a bet that the baseline is the product. If the app knows what’s normal for this one dog, it can say when something isn’t, and everyone who looks after her, the sitter, the vet, the groomer, can work from the same page. It’s a personal concept, started October 9, 2026. I did all of it: the research and positioning, the name, the brand and mascot, the wireframes, the UI, and a working React prototype.

Most pet apps are built for the owner. Snoot is built for everyone who looks after the dog.

23 · Her normal
0 × 0
1

Her normal, not a symptom list.

The decision that shaped everything else. Snoot never shows a list of possible causes; it compares what you’re seeing with her own history. “Third tummy issue this month” is useful. Twelve scary diagnoses at midnight is not.

24 · Context
0 × 0
2

Three next steps, never a diagnosis.

Every worry ends in one of three outcomes: keep watching, call your vet, or go now. Snoot says plainly that it isn’t a vet and that your gut counts. Drawing that line early kept the product honest and the scope sane.

25 · Sitter
0 × 0
3

The sitter never installs anything.

A sitter gets a link, not an app: routine, meds, quirks, emergency contacts, a to-do list. When breakfast gets checked off, you see it from the beach. Asking a sitter to download and sign up would have killed the feature on day one.

26 · Quiet
0 × 0
4

Quiet by default.

Reminders exist only for things you set up: vaccines, meds, checkups. No daily pings, no streaks. An app about a living animal has no business manufacturing urgency.

27 · Color
0 × 0
5

One red, and it means act.

Pixel is drawn in black line on cream, with no color at all. That keeps tomato red free for the moments you should act on, so “Something seems off?” is the only loud thing on the home screen.

28 · Building it

How it was built

I storyboarded Pixel’s life with the app before drawing a single screen: one moment at a time, from setup to the sitter’s check-offs. The storyboard decided which screens earned a place and which were just features. Then the flows, then the UI, then a working prototype in React, so the logic could be felt instead of described.

51 · When it goes wrong
0 × 0

When it goes wrong

People mistype things, and an app should help, not scold. The error states got the same care as the happy path, because the first time Snoot talks back to someone is usually the moment they got something wrong.

Specific email messages.“That’s missing an @, like you@email.com.” instead of “Invalid email.”
A password checklist.Each rule checks off as you type, so nobody has to guess.
Show and hide.One tap to see what you typed.
A way around the camera.No paperwork? Type it in by hand.
Basic mobile rules.44px tap targets, readable contrast, and room for the notch and the home bar.

56 · The brand
0 × 0

Your dog’s whole life, in one place. Snacks not included.

That’s the tagline on the welcome screen, and it sets the terms: warm, a little silly, and plain-spoken the moment it matters. The welcome animation makes the same promise. Cream goo fills the dog-shaped S, the S slides left, and N, O, O, T pop in one by one.

Why it looks like this: the product is about one specific dog, so the brand is a dog, not a logotype. Pixel in black line on cream keeps the whole app calm and leaves tomato as the one colour that means act. The type does the same split: DynaPuff for warmth and the big moments, Nunito for the words you read in a hurry at midnight.

94a · Go to market
0 × 0

How Snoot would reach people

It’s a concept, but I planned the launch the way I’d plan the product: who first, what the wedge is, how it pays for itself, and what I’d refuse to do. The short version: the sitter link is the growth loop, vets are the channel, and the owner is the only one who ever pays.

94b · Positioning
0 × 0

Positioning

Not another pet-records app. Snoot is the app that knows your dog’s normal. Everything else, records, reminders, the sitter guide, is in service of that one claim.

94c · Who first
0 × 0

Who first

First-year owners and owners of dogs with a chronic thing: allergies, seniors, the nervous ones. They’re the midnight-Googlers, and they already keep notes somewhere worse.

94d · The wedge
0 × 0

The wedge

Every sitter link puts Snoot in front of someone who doesn’t have it, with nothing to install. A trip is an invitation. That loop is why the sitter guide never requires an account.

94e · Channels
0 × 0

Channels

Vets and groomers first: the vet summary saves them time, so a counter card earns its place. Then breed communities (dachshunds, obviously) and honest answers in the “is this normal?” threads.

94f · How it pays
0 × 0

How it pays

Free forever: profile, check-ins, reminders, the sitter link. Snoot Plus for the things that cost money to run, paperwork scanning and vet summaries: $19 a year per household, or $49 once. No ads, no selling data. In a health app, trust is the product.

94g · What I’d measure
0 × 0

What I’d measure

Dogs with a 30-day baseline, weekly check-ins, sitter links opened, and whether a vet summary was used at a visit. Not downloads. A download isn’t a dog that’s better looked after.

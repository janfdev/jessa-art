Harbor started with a simple question: what if your bank felt like coming home instead?

Harbor is a mobile-first bank for everyday people in the US. It’s a design concept I built end to end, from the first wireframe to a clickable prototype, a marketing site, and a full set of brand assets.

How Harbor stands apart

Before designing anything, I looked at how dozens of banking apps greet new people, from big banks to newer apps like Chime, Monzo, Wise, and Cleo. A pattern jumped out. Almost every app picks one of two moods.

28 · Registration
0 × 0

Opening an account without the dread

US banks are required to confirm who you are before opening an account: legal name, birthday, address, Social Security number, and a photo of your ID. None of it can be skipped, so the goal was to make it feel lighter.

One question per screen.“What’s your phone number?” beats a wall of fields.
Four clear sections.About you, Your details, Verify it’s you, Set up your account, with a progress bar.
A heads-up before it starts.What to have ready, about five minutes, and progress saves.
A reason next to every sensitive ask.The SSN screen says it plainly: the law requires it, it won’t touch your credit score, and it’s encrypted.

37 · When it goes wrong
0 × 0

Four outcomes, not one

Identity checks don’t always pass on the first try. Approved, try again, under review, and couldn’t verify each get their own tone and next step, so nobody hits a dead end. The same thinking went into money.

Not enough money?You find out on the amount screen, not after you hit confirm.
Paying someone newtriggers a quick code, to stop scams before they happen.
Unclaimed moneystays in your account until they claim it, and comes back after 14 days.
A failing scheduled billwarns you a couple of days early, and the schedule stays active.

44 · Small details
0 × 0

Trust in the small details

Card controls one tap away.Freeze, view details, set limits, or report a card lost. Numbers show only after Face ID, then hide again on their own.
Loading screens that tell you what’s happening.Instead of a spinner, a little character shows the step.
Friendly errors.“That code didn’t match. Give it another try.” instead of “Error 401.”
Quick, familiar login.Face ID first, a passcode as backup, and a calm “no worries” screen if you forget your password.

48 · The brand
0 × 0

A brand that feels like a deep breath.

Money is emotional. People open a banking app when they’re excited, stressed, or just checking that everything’s okay. Every part of the brand is there to lower the heart rate a little. The voice is short sentences, plain words, and more “you” than “we.” Sea words show up now and then, never on every screen, so it stays charming instead of cheesy.

steadywarmsimplelightly playful
“Welcome home, money.”“Safely docked.”“No worries.”
49 · The cast
0 × 0

A cast, not a mascot

I tried puffy, blob, and low-poly before picking low-poly. It looked friendly without looking like a kids’ app, and the facets matched the card backgrounds. My first pass had a boat on every slide, which got old fast. So each moment got its own character instead, and suddenly it felt like a little world.

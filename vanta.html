13 · Storefront
0 × 0

A working storefront, not a landing page.

A filtered shop, product pages with size selection and specs, a slide-out cart with live quantity, and a full checkout. Mobile-first, with every grid reflowing cleanly. Enough interaction to feel alive, never enough to get in the way of buying.

14 · The brief
0 × 0

Most sportswear sites move the same way. VANTA was an exercise in the opposite.

Most direct-to-consumer sportswear sites run the same playbook: a stock hero slider, color spread thin across the page, safe system type, and a wall of product below the fold. Competent. Completely forgettable.

I wanted to build a brand from nothing and commit to one point of view, hard. What makes someone stop scrolling and feel something before they read a single word?

15 · The idea
0 × 0

Built to Move.

The line came first. The brand followed. It had to carry three very different people at once: the sprinter, the person on the mat, and someone walking the dog on a Sunday. So I built it around motion as a way of living, not a type of athlete.

16 · Colour

Colour.

Near-black, a warm off-white, and one electric orange as the only accent. Use it almost nowhere. One loud color used sparingly reads as confidence.

17 · Type
0 × 0

Typography.

A heavy condensed display face against a quiet grotesque. The display shouts at enormous sizes. The outlined word became the signature.

18 · Motion
0 × 0
3

Motion.

Saved for a few big moments: a black page-load wipe, staggered reveals, a slow parallax drift, product cards that stay still until touched. Sparse motion feels expensive.

19 · Layout

Layout.

22 · The film
0 × 0

Four sports. One frame.

The hero is a four-part cinematic reel that plays in sequence and loops. The discipline was in the lighting. Every clip shares one language: desaturated and high-contrast, deep shadow, a single warm rim of orange. Four separate scenes that read as one shoot. That consistency is what turns a pile of clips into a campaign.

36 · Why it works
0 × 0

The gap between competent and memorable isn’t budget. It’s a point of view, committed to fully.

VANTA picks an extreme: monochrome with one loud accent, oversized condensed type, motion held in reserve. Then it commits, down to the hairline. I took it from concept to a live build with an AI-assisted pipeline for the film and imagery, fast enough to see, test, and refine an idea the same day.
